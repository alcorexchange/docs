[Alcor Signer](https://sign.alcor.exchange) is a wallet in the browser. The key is a **passkey** on the person's
device (Face ID, Touch ID, Windows Hello, an Android fingerprint or a security key), so there is no extension to
install, no app and no seed phrase to type in. It works on **Telos, WAX, XPR Network and Vaulta** (formerly EOS) with
one passkey for all four.

It lives on its own site, `sign.alcor.exchange`, apart from any app that uses it. An app opens the Signer in a
window, the person sees what they are signing there and confirms with a click and their passkey. The app never
holds the key, and code injected into the app cannot sign with it: the browser only hands the passkey to the site
it was made for, and the chain checks that site inside every signature.

## What a dApp gets

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Sign in** | The person picks one of their accounts in the Signer window. You get the account and its permission. |
| **Sign transactions** | You send actions; the Signer builds the transaction from its own nodes, shows it in plain words, and returns it signed. Every transaction opens the window and takes a click. |
| **Prove an account** | The person signs a message with your statement (Sign-In with Antelope). Verify it on your server or in a contract to know who controls the account. |
| **Networks** | Telos, WAX, XPR Network, Vaulta. |

What a dApp does **not** get, by design:

- **No signing of raw bytes or digests.** A signed digest can be a transaction. Use [proofs](/alcor-signer/prove-account) instead.
- **No permission changes.** `updateauth`, `deleteauth`, `linkauth`, `unlinkauth`, `canceldelay`, `setcode`, `setabi`
  and every `eosio.msig` action are refused for any app. The person changes their keys inside the Signer only.
- **No Auto sign and no iframe.** Signing without a window is for Alcor's own trading app. A partner's site is
  never framed and always gets the window.
- **No resources.** Your dApp pays its own CPU and NET.

## Get your site listed

The Signer answers only the sites on its list. Requests from any other origin are ignored, and the plugin reports
that the window *did not open in time, or does not accept requests from* your site.

To get listed, **write to the Alcor team in Telegram, [t.me/alcorexchange](https://t.me/alcorexchange)**, with:

- your exact origins: production and staging, with `https://` and the port if it is not 443
  (`https://app.example.com`, `https://staging.example.com:8443`);
- the networks you use and a line on what the dApp does.

A local Signer for development answers `https://localhost:4000` without listing: see
[local development](/alcor-signer/wharfkit-plugin#local-development).

## How it protects the person

- **What you see is what you sign.** The app sends only actions. The Signer builds the transaction (chain id,
  reference block, expiration) and draws the screen from the transaction it signs, not from the request.
- **Fixed networks.** An app only names a network. Nodes and chain ids are built into the Signer; a node that
  reports another chain id is a refusal.
- **Checked contracts.** ABIs are pinned by hash; one that does not match must be confirmed by a second node, and
  the screen warns.
- **Readable screens.** Swaps, orders, transfers, bridge moves and staking are shown in plain words; a missing
  minimum, a foreign recipient or an unknown memo is shown in red or yellow, never hidden.
- **Clicks only.** No hotkeys, no autofocus on the sign button, and the button ignores clicks right after the window
  gets focus.
- **Recovery.** The passkey derives 24 words (BIP-39) that bring the accounts back if the passkey is lost.
