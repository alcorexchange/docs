---
title: "How to Use the Alcor Price Oracle"
description: "Reading TWAP prices from Alcor Swap pools in your own contract."
---

The interface and an example contract are in [alcorexchange/alcor-oracle-price](https://github.com/alcorexchange/alcor-oracle-price) on GitHub.

1. Copy the `include` folder of the repository into your contract project. It reads the `pools` and `observations` tables of `swap.alcor` directly, so no call to Alcor is needed.
2. Include `alcorpriceoracle_interface.hpp` and call:

   ```cpp
   uint128_t priceX64 = AlcorPriceOracle::getPriceTwapX64(poolId, twapInterval);
   ```

   - `poolId`: the Alcor Swap pool to read.
   - `twapInterval`: the averaging window in seconds. `0` returns the current spot price.
   - The result is the price of the pool's `tokenA` in `tokenB`, in raw token units (without decimals), as a Q64.64 fixed-point number: divide by 2^64 to get the ratio.

3. Make sure the pool keeps enough history for your window. `AlcorPriceOracle::getOldestObservationSecondsAgo(poolId)` returns the age of the oldest stored observation. If it is shorter than your window, [buy more observations](/alcor-swap/alcorswap-v2-price-oracles/expanding-price-availability-with-ram-purchase).

The interface also has `consult(poolId, secondsAgo)`, which returns the tick change and the harmonic mean liquidity over a window.

## Choosing the window

Many protocols use a 30-minute TWAP. A longer window is harder to manipulate but follows the market more slowly during volatility. A shorter window tracks the market more closely but is cheaper to manipulate. Choose according to your protocol's risks, and use a pool with deep liquidity.
