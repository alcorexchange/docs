The Alcor swap router reads `book.alcor` together with the AMM pools. When your quotes beat a pool, swaps fill
against them — so liquidity in the book reaches every swap on Alcor, not only traders on the order book page.

## Quote with one transaction

Update a market with one transaction: first `cancel` the orders that moved, then `placeorder` the new ones. Actions run
in order, so the funds the cancels release are already available to the new orders.

- **Flag 8, post-only-skip.** If the price moves while your transaction is on its way, a quote that would now
  cross the book is simply not placed, and the rest of the batch goes through. With plain post-only (`1`) one crossing
  quote reverts the whole transaction — including the cancels, which leaves your old quotes standing exactly when the
  price has run.
- **`expires_at`.** Give every quote an expiry, for example 60 seconds ahead in block time. If your bot stops, its
  quotes stop trading on their own. Refresh them well before they expire: the router treats an order as expired
  10 seconds early.
- **Requote threshold.** Leave an order in place while its price is within a few basis points of where you want it.
  Every update costs CPU, and a new order loses its place in the queue.
- **Start and stop with `cancelall`.** It removes up to 100 orders per call; call again while it returns 100.

## Fills

Subscribe to your account on the [order book API](/api/order-book#websocket):

```json
{"op": "subscribe", "channel": "account:alice"}
```

A `trade` event where `owner` is your account is a fill of your order: `id` is the order, `qty` the lots filled,
`qty_rem` what is left. The events of your own transactions also come back in the transaction result
(`KeySigner.transact` in the [SDK](/developers-api/order-book/sdk)).

## Reading your own state

A node in `read-mode = head` shows a transaction only once a producer's block includes it. Reading your orders right
after sending can return the state from before. Wait until the orders your transaction opened appear and the ones it
cancelled disappear — the transaction's `order_open` and `order_cancel` events list them — or until the head is a few
blocks past the transaction's block.

## Things to know

- **Self-trade prevention.** Your own order in the way of your taker order is cancelled, not traded.
- **Buy locks include the fee.** A buy locks `ceil(qty * price * (1 + fee_bps / 10000))`; check it against your
  available quote with `buyReserve`.
- **Dust.** A maker remainder below `min_notional` after a partial fill is cancelled and refunded.
- **Swaps sell whole lots.** A swap of base sells `floor(amount / lot_size)` lots and returns the rest, so a large
  lot means larger change for swappers.

## CPU

Cancelling and placing about eight orders in one transaction costs roughly 1–1.6 ms of CPU. Re-quoting a market
every few seconds adds up to several seconds of CPU a day per market.

| Network | How to get CPU |
| --- | --- |
| Telos | Stake TLOS to the account |
| WAX | Stake WAX, or have another account pay: on WAX the first authorizer of the first action pays for the transaction, so a payer's permission linked to a no-op action can go first in every transaction |
| XPR Network | `newaccres` gives a new account about 3 s a day. More comes as a 31-day plan from the `resources` contract: transfer XPR to `resources` (memo `account`), then `resources::buyplan(account, plan_index, plan_quantity)`. The plans are in its `plans` table; Plus (1000 XPR) gives about 320 s a day |

On XPR, `eosio::stakexpr` is a voting stake and does not add CPU.
